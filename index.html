Compute in Transit

Moving data now bounds much of LLM training and serving. Optalysys has demonstrated a photonic link module that computes on data as it crosses a link: FFT, NTT, modular arithmetic and MAC, in a QSFP56 pluggable. Three decks ask which LLM data movements it could work on, how much work each needs per byte, and why the strongest candidate is in training, not inference.

The idea and the hardware

Compute in transit
Computation applied in the data path, on data that must move anyway, instead of fetching it to a processor and sending it back. It helps only where the transfer bounds the system.Explained in: CiT 01 · slide 02
Placement: endpoint, link, switch
Where the work sits decides what it can see: an endpoint sees its own data, a pluggable link module sees one flow at line rate, and a switch sees many flows, so only a switch can combine streams.Explained in: CiT 01 · slide 02
Photonic link module
Optalysys's QSFP56 pluggable with an integrated optical Fourier transform device, reported to run FFT, NTT, modular arithmetic and MAC on link traffic at “tens of GFLOPs” (the authors' claim, evaluated on CKKS bootstrapping; arXiv:2608.21536).Explained in: CiT 01 · slide 03
Published operation set
The operations the public sources list for the module: FFT, NTT, modular arithmetic and MAC. Comparisons, rounding, top-k selection and data-dependent control are not listed, so a kernel must apply the same arithmetic to every value.Explained in: CiT 01 · slide 04
Recombined optical transforms
Small, low-precision on-die optical transforms (4 points per stage in Optalysys's 2023 description) combined into larger, higher-precision power-of-2 FFTs or NTTs.Explained in: CiT 01 · slide 05
Operations per byte
A link module's compute rate divided by its line rate: the arithmetic it can apply to each byte without slowing the link. 0.40 to 3.96 on a QSFP56 link, reading “tens of GFLOPs” as 10 to 99 GFLOP/s.Explained in: CiT 01 · slide 06; CiT 02 · slide 05
Transform charged or free
Whether the optical transform's FLOPs count against the budget. Charged, only BF16 FFTs up to length 8 fit; free, every fixed-band kernel fits and only the remaining arithmetic counts. Public sources do not say which.Explained in: CiT 01 · slide 07
Noise against exactness
Floating-point traffic needs only a noise level that matches its format's own rounding error; cryptography needs exact results, which the optics reaches by decomposition into smaller exact pieces.Explained in: CiT 01 · slide 08
Link energy per bit
Energy to move one bit over a link. Optalysys estimates 2.76 pJ/bit for its 45 nm silicon photonics, excluding the laser; the simulator's whole-path link presets (3 to 15 pJ/bit) are illustrative and not directly comparable.Explained in: CiT 01 · slide 09
The five tests
Bottleneck (is the transfer what bounds the system), operations (built only from the published set), budget (fits the operations per byte), precision (tolerates noise or can be exact) and placement (one flow or many).Explained in: CiT 01 · slide 10; CiT 03 · slide 11
Transport-bound
A system whose time or throughput is set by a link, the only case in which compressing that link's traffic helps. In the simulator the KV hand-off bound serving only over slow links near saturation.Explained in: CiT 01 · slide 10; CiT 03 · slide 03

Training traffic

The traffic of each kind of parallelism
Data parallel moves gradients; FSDP/ZeRO moves weights and gradients; tensor and pipeline parallel move activations; ring attention moves KV blocks; expert parallel moves tokens.Explained in: CiT 02 · slide 01
FSDP and ZeRO
Each worker holds a shard of the weights, gradients and optimiser state; it all-gathers the weights before use and reduce-scatters the gradients after the backward pass.Explained in: CiT 02 · slide 01
Ring all-reduce
A reduce-scatter then an all-gather round a ring of N workers; each sends 2(N−1)/N times the buffer, close to twice the buffer whatever N is. The arithmetic is one add per value.Explained in: CiT 02 · slide 03
In-network aggregation
Summing workers' updates inside switches (SwitchML on programmable switches, reporting up to 5.5× training speed-up; SHARP in InfiniBand). The flows must meet, so it belongs in a switch, not in a module on one link.Explained in: CiT 02 · slide 05; CiT 03 · slide 08
Low-communication training (DiLoCo family)
Workers take many local optimiser steps, then exchange a pseudo-gradient and apply one outer step. DiLoCo, Streaming DiLoCo, MuLoCo and SparseLoCo cut how often and how much is sent, for training across data centres.Explained in: CiT 02 · slide 06
Error feedback
Keeping the part of an update that compression discarded and adding it back next time, so the error is delayed rather than lost.Explained in: CiT 02 · slide 06
Frequency-domain gradient compression
Transforming a gradient or momentum tensor (FFT or DCT) and sending only some coefficients, on the premise that its energy sits in a few of them (Wang et al., HPDC 2020).Explained in: CiT 02 · slide 07
DeMo (decoupled momentum)
Each worker keeps its own momentum, applies a DCT to it in chunks, sends the top-k components and subtracts what it sent; the paper reports up to 85× less data per GPU than AdamW-DDP at comparable loss (ICLR 2026).Explained in: CiT 02 · slide 08
Fixed band in place of top-k
Keeping a fixed set of transform coefficients instead of the largest ones: a gather with no comparisons, so it suits the published operations, but it keeps the energy only if the energy sits in that band.Explained in: CiT 01 · slide 04; CiT 02 · slide 09
The fixed-band experiment
The planned test: DeMo with top-k replaced by a fixed frequency band, with the optics emulated, measured as validation loss against bytes communicated.Explained in: CiT 02 · slide 10

Inference traffic

KV bytes per token
A model's KV cache per prompt token: 131,072 bytes for Llama-3-8B and 327,680 for Llama-3-70B in BF16, so hand-offs grow linearly with the prompt.Explained in: CiT 03 · slide 02
KV hand-off
Moving a request's KV cache from the prefill pool that built it to the decode pool that uses it, in disaggregated serving.Explained in: CiT 03 · slide 03
Frequency keep-k KV compression
Transforming KV along the token axis and keeping a fixed band of components (FreqKV keeps half by default): an FFT and a gather, inside the published operations.Explained in: CiT 03 · slide 04
KV-cache quantisation
Storing KV at 2 to 4 bits (KIVI, KVQuant). It needs block maxima and rounding, which are outside the published operation set.Explained in: CiT 03 · slide 04
KV tiering and prefix caching
Keeping KV in CPU memory, SSD or a shared pool and fetching it back when a prefix is reused (LMCache, Mooncake): the series' best inference candidate.Explained in: CiT 03 · slide 05
Query-aware KV selection
Scoring page summaries against the query and fetching only the top pages (Quest). Scoring is MAC, 1 operation per BF16 byte; ranking needs comparisons.Explained in: CiT 03 · slide 06
Photonic similarity engine (PRISM)
A proposed photonic engine, by other authors, that ranks KV blocks by inner product at low precision: evidence that KV selection is being explored optically.Explained in: CiT 03 · slide 06
Ring attention (context parallelism)
Splitting a long sequence across devices and passing KV blocks round a ring at every layer, overlapped with blockwise exact attention. Its transfers are usually hidden behind compute.Explained in: CiT 02 · slide 01; CiT 03 · slide 07
MoE all-to-all
Expert parallelism sends each token's activation to the device holding its expert and back, every MoE layer.Explained in: CiT 02 · slide 01; CiT 03 · slide 08
ML-KEM (FIPS 203)
NIST's lattice-based post-quantum key encapsulation. Its polynomial arithmetic uses NTTs, which suit the module, but handshakes are only kilobytes.Explained in: CiT 03 · slide 10
Link kernel
A simulator abstraction for work done on a link: it declares operations per byte, operation types, precision need, compression ratio and placement (pluggable or switch).Explained in: CiT 03 · slide 12
