Compute in Transit

The goal: compress gradient traffic on the wire without sending indices, with the compression done for free in the link. A fixed frequency band needs no comparisons, so the optics can compute it in transit, and both ends already know which coefficients are sent. What was found: at this scale, a negative result. A fixed band did not replace DeMo’s top-k (deck 04), and a pluggable module computing it never breaks even with AdamW in the simulation (deck 05); summing the band at the switch pays only on slow WANs.

Moving data now bounds much of LLM training and serving. A photonic link module (arXiv:2608.21536) has been demonstrated that computes on data as it crosses a link: FFT, NTT, MAC, and modular and floating-point arithmetic, in a QSFP56 pluggable. Three decks ask which LLM data movements it could work on, how much work each needs per byte, and why the strongest candidate is in training, not inference; a fourth tests that candidate by experiment, and a fifth simulates what it means in time and energy.

The idea and the hardware

Compute in transit
Computation applied in the data path, on data that must move anyway, instead of fetching it to a processor and sending it back. It helps only where the transfer bounds the system.Explained in: CiT 01 · slide 02
Placement: endpoint, link, switch
Where the work sits decides what it can see: an endpoint sees its own data, a pluggable link module sees one flow at line rate, and a switch sees many flows, so only a switch can combine streams.Explained in: CiT 01 · slide 02; CiT 05 · slide 11
Photonic link module
A QSFP56 pluggable with an integrated optical Fourier transform device, reported to run FFT, NTT, MAC, and modular and floating-point arithmetic on link traffic at “tens of GFLOPs” (the authors' claim, evaluated on CKKS bootstrapping; arXiv:2608.21536).Explained in: CiT 01 · slide 03; its signal path, animated: FOptInf 01 · slide 15; hardware primer (modulators, coherent detection, constellations) → FOptInf 04; the future stacked chip and which AI workloads it could run → FOptInf 05
Published operation set
The operations the paper lists for the module: radix-4 FFT and NTT, addition, subtraction, multiplication, MAC, floating-point MAC and modular arithmetic. Comparisons, rounding, top-k selection and data-dependent control are not listed, so a kernel must apply the same arithmetic to every value.Explained in: CiT 01 · slide 04
Recombined optical transforms
Small, low-precision on-die optical transforms (four optical channels with radix-4 decomposition in the prototype; Kundu et al.) combined into larger, higher-precision power-of-2 FFTs or NTTs.Explained in: CiT 01 · slide 05
Slab waveguide (OFT)
The module’s optical Fourier transform (OFT): a passive diffractive slab (the paper’s “passive diffractive device”; “a slab waveguide” in its patent EP4677399A1), not a mesh of Mach-Zehnder interferometers, with no phase shifters inside. Its cost is insertion loss (1.977 dB in the stacked-chip simulator, plms 0.5.0), which lowers margins and the units one laser can feed (12 against a lossless mesh’s 20) but does not change the verdict on the future stacked chip.Explained in: FOptInf 01 · slide 16; FOptInf 05 · slide 01b; Fourier Optics glossary
Native size N
How many points one slab transforms in a pass: 4 on the module. A larger N cuts the stages of a long FFT, but beyond 4 points the outputs of a DFT of bits are not integers, so only N = 4 keeps exact (FHE) arithmetic; a larger N helps only an approximate mode (3.092 effective bits fully chained at N = 64 in the simulator).Explained in: FOptInf 05 · slide 16b
Operations per byte
A link module's compute rate divided by its line rate: the arithmetic it can apply to each byte without slowing the link. 0.40 to 3.96 on a QSFP56 link, reading “tens of GFLOPs” as 10 to 99 GFLOP/s. At the prototype optics’ own rate (four modulators at 14 GBaud NRZ, about 7 GB/s) it is 1.43 to 14.14, on a link the prototype could not fill.Explained in: CiT 01 · slide 06; CiT 02 · slide 05
Transform charged or free
Whether the optical transform's FLOPs count against the budget. Charged, only BF16 FFTs up to length 8 fit; free, every fixed-band kernel fits and only the remaining arithmetic counts. Public sources do not say which.Explained in: CiT 01 · slide 07; CiT 05 · slide 04
Noise against exactness
Floating-point traffic needs only a noise level that matches its format's own rounding error; cryptography needs exact results, which the optics reaches by decomposition into smaller exact pieces.Explained in: CiT 01 · slide 08
Link energy per bit
Energy to move one bit over a link. The module’s developer estimates 2.76 pJ/bit for its 45 nm silicon photonics, excluding the laser; the simulator's whole-path link presets (3 to 15 pJ/bit) are illustrative and not directly comparable.Explained in: CiT 01 · slide 09; CiT 05 · slide 09
The five tests
Bottleneck (is the transfer what bounds the system), operations (built only from the published set), budget (fits the operations per byte), precision (tolerates noise or can be exact) and placement (one flow or many).Explained in: CiT 01 · slide 10; CiT 03 · slide 11
Transport-bound
A system whose time or throughput is set by a link, the only case in which compressing that link's traffic helps. In the simulator the KV hand-off bound serving only over slow links near saturation.Explained in: CiT 01 · slide 10; CiT 03 · slide 03; CiT 05 · slide 06

Training traffic

The traffic of each kind of parallelism
Data parallel moves gradients; FSDP/ZeRO moves weights and gradients; tensor and pipeline parallel move activations; ring attention moves KV blocks; expert parallel moves tokens.Explained in: CiT 02 · slide 01
FSDP and ZeRO
Each worker holds a shard of the weights, gradients and optimiser state; it all-gathers the weights before use and reduce-scatters the gradients after the backward pass.Explained in: CiT 02 · slide 01
Ring all-reduce
A reduce-scatter then an all-gather round a ring of N workers; each sends 2(N−1)/N times the buffer, close to twice the buffer whatever N is. The arithmetic is one add per value.Explained in: CiT 02 · slide 03; CiT 04 · slide 03; CiT 05 · slide 02
In-network aggregation
Summing workers' updates inside switches (SwitchML on programmable switches, reporting up to 5.5× training speed-up; SHARP in InfiniBand). The flows must meet, so it belongs in a switch, not in a module on one link.Explained in: CiT 02 · slide 05; CiT 03 · slide 08; CiT 05 · slide 11
Low-communication training (DiLoCo family)
Workers take many local optimiser steps, then exchange a pseudo-gradient and apply one outer step. DiLoCo, Streaming DiLoCo, MuLoCo and SparseLoCo cut how often and how much is sent, for training across data centres.Explained in: CiT 02 · slide 06; CiT 05 · slide 10
Error feedback
Keeping the part of an update that compression discarded and adding it back next time, so the error is delayed rather than lost.Explained in: CiT 02 · slide 06; CiT 04 · slide 08; CiT 04 · slide 09
Frequency-domain gradient compression
Transforming a gradient or momentum tensor (FFT or DCT) and sending only some coefficients, on the premise that its energy sits in a few of them (Wang et al., HPDC 2020).Explained in: CiT 02 · slide 07
DeMo (decoupled momentum)
Each worker keeps its own momentum, applies a DCT to it in chunks, sends the top-k components and subtracts what it sent; the paper reports up to 85× less data per GPU than AdamW-DDP at comparable loss (ICLR 2026).Explained in: CiT 02 · slide 08; CiT 04 · slide 01
Fixed band in place of top-k
Keeping a fixed set of transform coefficients instead of the largest ones: a gather with no comparisons, so it suits the published operations, but it keeps the energy only if the energy sits in that band.Explained in: CiT 01 · slide 04; CiT 02 · slide 09; CiT 04 · slide 04
The fixed-band experiment
DeMo with top-k replaced by a fixed frequency band, with the optics emulated, measured as validation loss against bytes communicated. On a 15.8M-parameter GPT every fixed band ended more than a nat above top-k (top-k 1.785, AdamW 1.646, the low and calibrated bands 3.087 to 3.158), and top-k reached the 1-D band’s final loss in 400 steps.Explained in: CiT 02 · slide 10 (the plan); CiT 04 · slide 04; CiT 04 · slide 05

The fixed-band experiment

Payload and wire bytes
Payload is what a worker originates each step; wire bytes are what it sends in its ring collective. An all-reduce sends about twice its buffer whatever the number of workers; an all-gather, which DeMo needs because each worker’s top-k positions differ, sends the payload once to each other worker. With eight workers, DeMo’s 8× and a fixed band’s 16× payload savings are 2.0× and 4.0× on the wire.Explained in: CiT 04 · slide 03; CiT 05 · slide 03
Captured energy
The share of a tensor’s energy that a selector keeps at a given number of coefficients: the measure of whether a fixed band can stand in for top-k. In DeMo’s 2-D chunks the fixed low band kept 0.013 of the momentum’s energy, about a fifth of a random set, against 0.643 for top-k.Explained in: CiT 04 · slide 06
Residual
What error feedback keeps: everything compressed away and not yet sent, added back to the next step’s update and decaying slowly. With a fixed band the band is removed every step, so the residual grows out of band until the band is a few parts in 100,000 of the energy DeMo compresses.Explained in: CiT 04 · slide 06; CiT 04 · slide 08
Bit planes
Feeding an integer through a linear optical transform one binary digit at a time, as on-off (NRZ) light, then recombining the outputs with weights 2b. Each plane is one optical pass, so more planes mean more precision and more time.Explained in: CiT 04 · slide 07; CiT 04 · slide 10; CiT 05 · slide 08
ENOB
Effective number of bits: the precision a noisy analogue readout actually delivers, whatever its converter’s nominal width. In the experiment, ENOB 8 per plane was enough for the band once errors were fed back; ENOB 6 cost 0.3 in loss. The Fourier Optics glossary covers the noise rule behind it.Explained in: CiT 04 · slide 07; CiT 04 · slide 10; CiT 05 · slide 08
Feedback of the values sent (all-gather feedback)
Error feedback that subtracts what the optics actually produced, noise and all, rather than the exact band. It is possible because an all-gather returns every worker’s values, its own included. Feeding back the exact band let the optics’ errors repeat uncorrected and every such run diverged; feeding back the values sent fixed it.Explained in: CiT 04 · slide 09; CiT 04 · slide 11

The training-transit simulation

Roofline compute time
Compute time estimated from the arithmetic alone: 6 × parameters × tokens per worker per step, divided by the device’s peak rate times an achieved fraction (MFU; 0.40 here, illustrative). It sets the part of a step that compression cannot shorten.Explained in: CiT 05 · slide 02
Pipelined stages: the slowest wins
The module as a link kernel passes a stream through its port (25 GB/s), its optics (4 × 14 Gb/s NRZ, one pass per bit plane), its compute budget and an FPGA stage, all pipelined, so the slowest stage sets the rate. With the transform charged the budget binds below 140.0 GFLOP/s; with it free, the optics binds.Explained in: CiT 05 · slide 04; CiT 05 · slide 06
Hot spot
The resource that holds the largest share of a step, with each round of a sync charged to its slowest operation. AdamW’s step across two data centres is mostly the inter-DC link; a module with its transform charged holds 95.0% of the band’s step at 10 GFLOP/s.Explained in: CiT 05 · slide 06
Break-even
The value of one parameter (WAN bandwidth, module GFLOP/s or ENOB) at which two variants reach the target loss in the same time, found by bisection; “none in range” when one is faster throughout. A pluggable band never breaks even with AdamW or top-k over 0.1 to 1,000 GB/s.Explained in: CiT 05 · slide 07; CiT 05 · slide 08
In-switch summing with loopback
The band computed and summed by modules at the switch, so only the band sum crosses the WAN, with each worker’s own optical band sent back to it so that the fix for the optics’ errors (feedback of the values sent) still works. Hypothetical: not run. It beats AdamW only below 1.57 GB/s of WAN (0.83 GB/s on the exact band’s steps), and the same sum done by the GPUs does better.Explained in: CiT 05 · slide 03; CiT 05 · slide 07; CiT 05 · slide 11
E’s steps or the exact band’s
The repaired optics band reached the target in fewer steps than the exact band, but from one seed per run: a hypothesis. So every in-transit result is given twice, with E’s steps and with the exact band’s (three seeds), and each crossing says which it uses.Explained in: CiT 05 · slide 05; CiT 05 · slide 07

Inference traffic

KV bytes per token
A model's KV cache per prompt token: 131,072 bytes for Llama-3-8B and 327,680 for Llama-3-70B in BF16, so hand-offs grow linearly with the prompt.Explained in: CiT 03 · slide 02
KV hand-off
Moving a request's KV cache from the prefill pool that built it to the decode pool that uses it, in disaggregated serving.Explained in: CiT 03 · slide 03
Frequency keep-k KV compression
Transforming KV along the token axis and keeping a fixed band of components (FreqKV applies a DCT and keeps half by default): a transform, which an FFT can compute, and a gather, inside the published operations.Explained in: CiT 03 · slide 04
KV-cache quantisation
Storing KV at 2 to 4 bits (KIVI, KVQuant). It needs block maxima and rounding, which are outside the published operation set.Explained in: CiT 03 · slide 04
KV tiering and prefix caching
Keeping KV in CPU memory, SSD or a shared pool and fetching it back when a prefix is reused (LMCache, Mooncake): the series' best inference candidate.Explained in: CiT 03 · slide 05
Query-aware KV selection
Scoring page summaries against the query and fetching only the top pages (Quest). A plain dot-product score is MAC, 1 operation per BF16 byte; Quest's own bound and the ranking both need comparisons.Explained in: CiT 03 · slide 06
Photonic similarity engine (PRISM)
A proposed photonic engine, by other authors, that ranks KV blocks by inner product at low precision: evidence that KV selection is being explored optically.Explained in: CiT 03 · slide 06
Ring attention (context parallelism)
Splitting a long sequence across devices and passing KV blocks round a ring at every layer, overlapped with blockwise exact attention. Its transfers are usually hidden behind compute.Explained in: CiT 02 · slide 01; CiT 03 · slide 07
MoE all-to-all
Expert parallelism sends each token's activation to the device holding its expert and back, every MoE layer.Explained in: CiT 02 · slide 01; CiT 03 · slide 08
ML-KEM (FIPS 203)
NIST's lattice-based post-quantum key encapsulation. Its polynomial arithmetic uses NTTs, which suit the module, but handshakes are only kilobytes.Explained in: CiT 03 · slide 10
Link kernel
A simulator abstraction for work done on a link: it declares operations per byte, operation types, precision need, compression ratio and placement (pluggable or switch).Explained in: CiT 03 · slide 12; CiT 05 · slide 04
