# Compute-in-transit budget analysis

Generated by `analysis/cit_budget.py` (x86_64, Python 3.12.3, NumPy 2.5.3). Every number in the CiT decks comes from this file.

Conventions: real FFT or DCT of length n = 2.5 log2 n FLOPs per value (FFTW's convention); a 2-D c x c transform = 2 x 2.5 log2 c per value. BF16 = 2 bytes per value. Model shapes and link presets come from Disaggregated_Inference_Sim's `disagg_sim.hardware`. The module's compute claim, "tens of GFLOPs" per link (Kundu et al., arXiv:2608.21536), is bracketed here by 10 and 99 GFLOP/s: our reading, not a measurement.

## 1. The budget: operations per byte at line rate

| Link | Line rate (GB/s) | Claim | Operations per byte |
|---|---|---|---|
| QSFP56 200G | 25 | low (10 GFLOP/s) | 0.40 |
| QSFP56 200G | 25 | high (99 GFLOP/s) | 3.96 |
| 400G | 50 | low (10 GFLOP/s) | 0.20 |
| 400G | 50 | high (99 GFLOP/s) | 1.98 |
| 800G | 100 | low (10 GFLOP/s) | 0.10 |
| 800G | 100 | high (99 GFLOP/s) | 0.99 |

* On a QSFP56 link the module can apply **0.40 to 3.96 operations per byte**. If an FFT is charged against that budget, no transform fits at the low end (even length 2 costs 1.25 per BF16 byte) and the longest that fits at the high end is length 8. If the optics performs the transform without charge, only the remaining arithmetic counts.

## 5. A fixed frequency band against top-k (synthetic signals, illustrative)

Share of each 64 x 64 chunk's energy kept after a 2-D DCT, keeping 1/16 of the coefficients: the largest (top-k, DeMo's choice) or a fixed low-frequency block of the same size (needs no comparisons). Synthetic signals only; whether real momentum behaves like the smooth or the noisy case is what the fixed-band experiment measures.

| Signal | Kept fraction | Top-k energy | Fixed band energy |
|---|---|---|---|
| white noise | 0.0625 | 0.325 | 0.062 |
| 2-D random walk (smooth) | 0.0625 | 0.995 | 0.957 |
| sparse spikes (1% non-zero) | 0.0625 | 0.320 | 0.062 |

* A fixed band matches top-k only when energy sits at low frequencies (the smooth case). On noise or spikes it keeps about its own share of the energy, far below top-k.

## 6. Headline

* A QSFP56 compute-in-transit module offers about 0.40-3.96 operations per byte. Summing streams (0.5 per byte) and dot-product scoring (1 per byte) fit only towards the high end of that range; transforms fit only if the optics does them without charge.
* The largest movements in LLM work are training syncs: a Llama-3-70B BF16 update is 141.10 GB, against 268.4 MB for a 2,048-token Llama-3-8B KV hand-off.
* A fixed frequency band is the comparison-free substitute for top-k. It works only if the momentum's energy is concentrated at low frequencies within each chunk, which the experiment must test on real tensors.
